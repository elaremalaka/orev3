**4. Ref classification**

Every ref is **substantive by default**.

Substantive refs include branch, remote-tracking, tag, notes, stash, replacement and custom refs; symbolic-ref relationships; any ref used by repository tooling or governance to select content, establish ancestry, publish, recover authoritative work or identify accepted evidence. An application-created ref becomes substantive whenever it serves such a role.

A ref may be classified as ephemeral application bookkeeping only when all of the following are established:

1. Its exact namespace and structural grammar match authenticated application code. Initial eligible namespace roots are solely:
   - `refs/codex/turn-diffs/captures/`
   - `refs/codex/turn-diffs/checkpoints/`
2. The producer, version, repository identity, task/scope identity and lifecycle operation are corroborated by evidence independent of the ref name.
3. Its available object target and type match the authenticated producer’s behavior and the relevant captured content. A matching object ID alone is insufficient.
4. The ref is not consumed as substantive authority by repository configuration, scripts, workflows or accepted governance.
5. Its creation, retention, replacement or deletion is consistent with the authenticated lifecycle, including scope and ordering.
6. No contrary evidence suggests manual spoofing, unauthorized mutation, cross-repository attribution, unexpected content or substantive use.

Namespace membership, timestamp-shaped names, UUID-shaped names, mtimes, familiar application branding and a currently installed binary are never sufficient individually or collectively without provenance and continuity evidence.

Unknown classification is **U**, not an ephemeral exemption. A demonstrated substantive use or unauthorized mutation prevents use of this exception. New namespaces, producer versions or materially changed lifecycle behavior require fresh review before eligibility.

**5. Exact applicability conditions**

A historical case is eligible for consideration only if every condition below holds:

- The application is expressly authorized under an EFFECTIVE-ACCEPTED amendment. The applying task identifies one repository, one missing-evidence incident, a bounded historical interval, affected observations and the exact proposed continuity claim.
- The missing original’s established locator, size/hash where available, capture method and historical requirement are preserved.
- Exact recovery has been attempted through a documented, bounded search of relevant accessible sources. Sources include original session/tool output, preserved snapshots and manifests, ref storage/reflogs, relevant application logs/storage and available backups. Inaccessible sources and search limitations are listed.
- “Unavailable” means unavailable within that documented scope. It does not mean permanently nonexistent. Exhaustion, elapsed time or repeated failures never independently earns PASS.
- Positive evidence establishes the complete substantive-ref boundary across the gap. Equality of HEAD, the working tree or the surviving ref set is insufficient.
- All available surrounding snapshots are retained unfiltered, with exact additions, removals, value updates and symbolic relationships reconciled.
- The evidence supports restricting the remaining uncertainty to ephemeral identity/lifecycle details. An unclassified historical ref or an incomplete substantive boundary cannot be assigned to the ephemeral class by assumption.

- The lost ref bytes are not themselves required inputs to the contemplated work—for example, as a historical content snapshot or qualification artifact.

A historical substantive boundary may be established by independently preserved complete substantive-ref output at the relevant observation, or by an authenticated earlier complete boundary plus sufficiently complete transaction evidence spanning the relevant gap. Partial branch listings, a digest of an unavailable full listing, or absence of reflog entries do not establish that boundary.

**7. PASS/FAIL/U and anti-abuse rules**

There are three distinct decisions:

- **Governance review:** whether these proposed rules are acceptable.
- **Case review:** whether one exact dossier satisfies an effective amendment.
- **Receiving preflight:** whether the separately authorized receiving task satisfies all applicable controls now.

None substitutes for another.

A case receives **PASS** only when every applicability condition and required evidence obligation is satisfied, all consequential adversarial cases are resolved, reviewer independence is authenticated and the exact bounded claim is supported. Author completion is never case PASS.

A case receives **FAIL** for an established contradiction or violation: unauthorized mutation, altered evidence, attempted substantive-ref exemption, fabricated recovery, prohibited self-review, intentional omission, or use outside the authorized scope. FAIL takes precedence over simultaneous U findings; all findings remain recorded.

A case receives **U** where necessary evidence, attribution, ordering, substantive completeness or independence cannot be established. Suspected unauthorized mutation blocks with U until resolved; established unauthorized mutation blocks with FAIL. “No proof of wrongdoing” is not positive continuity evidence.

Additional protections:

- No deleting/restoring refs, refreshing the index, resetting files or otherwise forcing an expected boundary.
- No creating an evidence gap deliberately and then invoking the exception.
- No search-budget exhaustion, repeated task creation or management preference as an acceptance criterion.
- No pooling several gaps into a general waiver.
- No copying one case’s acceptance to another repository, interval, producer or missing artifact.
- No laundering an exception through a checkpoint that omits its residual uncertainty.
- No replacing exact existing evidence with substitutes because the substitute is easier.
- No treating several copies of the same source as independent corroboration.
- No treating endpoint equality as proof that a mutate-and-restore sequence never occurred.

**DA1 — Authority-anchor admission invariant**

After this amendment becomes effective, **no evidence object may become an accepted future authority anchor for a claim whose later verification requires that object’s original bytes unless those exact bytes are durably retrievable and independently re-verifiable before the authority boundary is accepted.**

An authority anchor is an object actually relied upon to establish an accepted claim or boundary for later continuation. Its role is determined by the claim’s evidentiary dependencies, not its filename, storage location, namespace or an author’s preferred label.

The accepting package must identify:

- The exact claim and its temporal or transition scope.
- The raw objects on which later verification genuinely depends.
- The controls and gates that depend on those objects.
- Any accepted alternative representation that fully authenticates a claim without dependence on the original raw object.

An object cannot be designated “transient” to evade retention while its original bytes remain necessary to the accepted claim. Equally, merely observing an object, mentioning it in a narrative or capturing a command output does not automatically make it an authority anchor.

This rule does not require blanket archival of command outputs, ordinary progress observations, application refs or temporary artifacts. Existing explicit preservation duties remain in force.

**DA2 — Required anchor evidence and bindings**

For each object promoted to a raw-dependent authority anchor, retain the exact raw bytes and sufficient authenticated bindings to establish, as applicable:

- Exact byte count; LF count for text where relevant; SHA-256.
- Repository identity and relevant branch, HEAD and index identity.
- Producer and task/session provenance, including original record identity when message-based.
- Observation timestamp and the ordering evidence necessary for the claim.
- Predecessor or prior-boundary identity.
- Exact transition/delta evidence sufficient for the claim being anchored.
- Durable retrieval location, retrieval method, and evidence linking the retained object to its authenticated original.

Capture methods, command identities, object types, symbolic relationships or other contextual evidence remain required where the claim needs them. A field may be inapplicable only with an explicit, claim-specific explanation; omission cannot silently remove a dependency.

Hashes and counts establish identity checks. They do not recreate unavailable bytes, establish source authenticity by themselves, or prove semantic sufficiency.

A point-in-time observation may anchor only its supported point-in-time claim. It cannot anchor interval continuity without sufficient exact transition evidence. Matching endpoints do not exclude mutate-and-restore activity.

**DA3 — Meaning of durable retention and acceptance verification**

Durable retention means retention in an explicitly accepted location and under procedures that allow later authorized lanes to retrieve and independently verify the exact bytes throughout their required reliance.

A durable repository-backed evidence artifact, content-addressed evidence store or another explicitly accepted durable location may satisfy this requirement. The location must provide usable retrieval and preservation arrangements; a descriptive label or content-addressed identifier alone is insufficient.

Terminal scrollback, `/private/tmp`, ephemeral application state, or conversational history alone cannot satisfy durable retention. A persisted original session can establish source identity during authentication, but its mere existence is not proof of accepted durable retention.

Before accepting the boundary, the accepting reviewer or lane must:

1. Enumerate every raw object actually relied upon as a future authority anchor.
2. Retrieve each object from its proposed durable location.
3. Independently verify its exact identity and necessary provenance/boundary bindings.
4. Verify the retained transition evidence supports the claim’s scope.
5. Record the retrieval/verification result and exact dependencies.

A promise to archive later, a manifest without retrievable members, or the author’s statement that a file exists does not satisfy acceptance.

Retention must continue while accepted claims or gates require those bytes, subject to stronger preservation requirements. Migration must authenticate the destination before reliance on the source is relinquished. This rule neither requires indefinite archival of non-authoritative transient observations nor authorizes deletion of protected historical evidence.

**DA4 — Dependent U, FAIL precedence and bounded blocking**

Failure to retain or retrieve a required authority-anchor object yields **U for the dependent continuity claim or gate**. If an established violation or contradiction also exists, C1’s **FAIL precedence** remains controlling; the missing-evidence finding remains recorded.

The dependency assessment must include indirect dependencies. A package cannot avoid U by splitting, renaming or omitting the claim that consumes the missing anchor.

Missing anchor evidence does not automatically block unrelated work. Work is unrelated for this purpose only when its required claims and gates do not depend on the missing object or unresolved interval, its remaining prerequisites are satisfied, and it is separately authorized. Removing one evidentiary dependency does not remove an independently required control.

An overall preflight that requires an unresolved dependent control remains U, or FAIL where FAIL precedence applies. This clause supplies no blanket permission to proceed under a failed preflight.

Loss of a genuinely non-authoritative transient observation creates no new retention-based U by itself. If a later task newly requires that observation’s original bytes and they are unavailable, that later claim has missing proof; the observation cannot be retroactively promoted on the strength of its hash.

**DA5 — Prospective establishment of a new boundary**

A later independently authenticated checkpoint or continuation boundary may establish a **new prospective clean authority boundary** without depending on older non-anchor transient evidence, provided all of the following hold:

- The boundary has explicit compatible authorization and satisfies applicable independent review, acceptance, adoption and publication requirements.
- Its present state and required authority are independently authenticated.
- Every object required as a future raw-dependent anchor satisfies DA1–DA3.
- The new boundary states its exact effective start, supported claims and excluded earlier interval.
- Earlier unresolved claims, negative findings and limitations remain visible and unchanged.
- No required historical prerequisite is silently removed or treated as satisfied.

Future continuity may then be evaluated from that accepted boundary for claims whose valid dependency scope begins there.

This is **prospective rebasing of future continuity**, not retroactive repair of historical evidence. It does not establish what happened during an unresolved earlier interval, recover missing bytes, upgrade an earlier U, validate earlier unauthorized work, or prove exact continuity from an older boundary.

If a proposed gate still depends on the older interval, a newly authenticated endpoint is insufficient and that dependency remains U/FAIL. A boundary cannot be chosen to conceal contradictory evidence or evade investigation of an established violation.

This permission is prospective only. It does not establish a new boundary for the current CS23 case or displace C1 clause 9’s current-case requirements.
