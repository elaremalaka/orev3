6. Practicality and limits of the finding

Rigour: sufficient. Admission depends on authenticated positive evidence, exact authority, complete transitive dependencies and independent acceptance, not namespace trust or endpoint inference.

Historical narrowness: sufficient. The design separates the existence and authority of current contracts from unresolved historical execution truth. It neither makes the missing listing harmless nor revalidates earlier unavailable prerequisites.

Operational practicality: sufficient at this governance-design stage. Collection is bounded by the complete current substantive state and exact exported claims. Excluded classes can be established without archiving every transient instance. Relevant state must be made coherently observable or supported by adequate transition evidence. Ordinary churn that is proved outside claim dependencies does not become an eternal archival debt. If uncontrolled relevant writers make a particular collection incoherent, that attempted boundary must stop; this is a real evidence defect, not structural impossibility of the protocol.

Non-recursion: sufficient for the targeted failure mode. A finite accepted N0 package and retained authorized deltas can support later checkpoints without re-demanding unrelated pre-N0 transient bookkeeping. The protocol does not guarantee that no future actor can lose necessary evidence or introduce a new backward dependency; those failures appropriately block affected claims. Stopping them is not a defect requiring weaker governance.

This PASS does not certify that a concrete N0 can presently be accepted, that the entire CD transitive closure is clear, or that an effective successor CS revision already exists.
